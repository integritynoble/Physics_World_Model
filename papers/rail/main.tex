\begin{abstract}
The computational imaging community has built increasingly powerful
reconstruction algorithms, yet real-world deployments routinely fail.
We show that a sub-pixel operator mismatch---a 0.5-pixel mask shift
with 0.1\textdegree{} rotation and 1\% dispersion drift, well within
manufacturing tolerances---degrades the state-of-the-art coded aperture snapshot spectral imaging (CASSI)
transformer (MST-L) by 13.98\,dB, erasing more than a decade of
algorithmic progress.
This paper argues that the bottleneck is not the solver but the
infrastructure around it: evaluation protocols, physics representations,
calibration pipelines, and benchmarks.
Drawing on the SolveEverything.org framework, we present the
\textbf{Physics World Model (PWM)} as the ``rail'' for computational
imaging---a standardized evaluation harness comprising:
(i)~OperatorGraph intermediate representation (IR), a universal directed acyclic graph (DAG) representation spanning 64
modalities across 5 physical carriers with 89 validated templates;
(ii)~a 4-scenario evaluation protocol separating solver quality from
operator fidelity;
(iii)~the Leaderboard for Imaging Physics (LIP-Arena), a prospective Commit-Measure-Score competition
eliminating benchmark overfitting; and
(iv)~a Red Team adversarial verification module.
Across a 26-modality benchmark, we demonstrate that operator correction
improves reconstruction by $+0.54$ to $+48.25$\,dB across 9 correction configurations spanning 7 distinct modalities, with mismatch (Gate~3) identified as the binding constraint
in every modality tested.\footnote{With full cross-solver validation for CASSI; other modalities assessed by absolute mismatch magnitude.}
PWM provides the infrastructure to move computational imaging from
artisanal practice to industrial standardization.
\end{abstract}
