## One-Sentence Summary (Nature format)

Two theorems establish that every computational imaging forward model decomposes into a directed acyclic graph over exactly 11 physically typed primitives and that operator mismatch --- not information deficiency or noise --- is the dominant reconstruction bottleneck across seven modalities spanning four carrier families, with forward-model correction recovering +0.8 to +10.7 dB on five real instruments.

## Table of Contents Summary (150 words)

Computational imaging reconstructs scenes by inverting a mathematical model of the measurement physics, yet this model inevitably diverges from reality. Here we prove that every imaging forward model in a broad operator class admits an approximate representation as a directed acyclic graph over exactly 11 canonical primitives --- a basis that is both sufficient and minimal. We further prove that every reconstruction failure decomposes into three root causes: information deficiency, carrier noise, and operator mismatch. Validating both results across seven modalities spanning optical photons, X-ray photons, electrons, and nuclear spins, we find that operator mismatch universally dominates: correcting the forward model recovers +0.8 to +10.7 dB of peak signal-to-noise ratio on five real instruments (Cohen's d > 2.0 for all modalities), routinely exceeding the gains of a decade of solver innovation. These results establish calibration, not algorithm design, as the systematically underinvested component of computational imaging.
