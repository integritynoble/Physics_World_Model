\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We demonstrate that large language model (LLM) agents can design arbitrary
computational imaging systems from natural language descriptions, with
formal guarantees on the approximation error of the resulting forward
model.  Our approach rests on two foundations:
(i)~the \emph{Finite Primitive Basis Theorem} (FPB Theorem), which proves
that any imaging forward model across 168+ modalities and 5 carrier
families can be decomposed into a directed acyclic graph (DAG) over
11 canonical primitives with representation error $\eps < 0.01$; and
(ii)~a \emph{Constrained Primitive Compiler} that validates every
agent-generated design is a legal composition within this basis.
A three-agent pipeline --- Plan, Judge, and Performance --- translates
user intent into formally typed operator graphs, validates physical and
algorithmic feasibility, and quantifies expected reconstruction quality.
We evaluate the system on 36 modalities spanning X-ray computed
tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, optical coherence tomography,
structured illumination microscopy, and 32 additional modalities.
A four-scenario validation protocol measures the recovery ratio~$\rho$,
quantifying how well auto-correction compensates for model mismatch
between the agent-designed and true systems.  Across all tested
modalities, agent-designed forward models achieve 100\% compilation
pass rate and 86.1\% canonical chain fidelity, establishing that
LLM-based system design is both general and formally grounded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Computational imaging systems --- from medical CT and MRI to
super-resolution microscopy and spectral cameras --- share a common
mathematical structure: a forward model~$A$ that maps an unknown
object~$x$ to measurements $y = A(x) + \text{noise}$.  Designing the
forward model for a new imaging system requires deep expertise in wave
optics, detector physics, signal processing, and modality-specific
domain knowledge.  This expertise bottleneck limits how quickly new
imaging modalities can be prototyped, validated, and deployed.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have shown that AI
agents can generate, refine, and validate complex technical designs
through multi-step reasoning\cite{jumper2021alphafold,merchant2023gnome}.
However, applying LLMs to physical system design faces a fundamental
challenge: \textbf{how to guarantee that the generated system is
physically valid and its approximation error is bounded}.

We address this challenge by combining two key ideas:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{The Finite Primitive Basis Theorem} establishes that any
  Tier-2 imaging forward model can be decomposed into a DAG of exactly
  11 canonical primitives --- Propagate~(P), Modulate~(M), Project~($\Pi$),
  Encode~(F), Convolve~(C), Accumulate~($\Sigma$), Detect~(D), Sample~(S),
  Disperse~(W), Scatter~(R), and Transform~($\Lambda$) --- with representation
  error $\eps < 0.01$, validated across 168+ modalities and 5 carrier
  families (photon, electron, acoustic, spin, particle).

\item \textbf{A Constrained Primitive Compiler} acts as a formal
  verification layer between the LLM agent and the executable forward
  model.  The compiler validates that every agent output is a legal DAG
  over the 11-primitive basis, satisfies node-count and depth bounds,
  and respects the nonlinear family constraints on the three nonlinear
  primitives (D, R, $\Lambda$).  Any design that passes compilation is
  automatically guaranteed to inherit the error bound from the FPB~Theorem.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting system enables a user to describe an imaging system in
plain English --- ``design a coded-aperture snapshot spectral imager
operating at 400--700\,nm'' --- and receive a formally validated,
executable forward model that can be directly used for simulation
and reconstruction.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Constrained Primitive Compiler} with 6 validation gates:
  DAG acyclicity, canonical chain matching, $N_\text{max}$/$D_\text{max}$
  bounds, nonlinear family constraints (5 families $\times$ $\leq$2
  parameters each for D and $\Lambda$), adjoint consistency, and
  representation error estimation.

\item \textbf{Three-agent pipeline} (Plan/Judge/Performance) that
  translates natural language to typed operator graphs through structured
  generation, feasibility validation, and performance prediction.

\item \textbf{Four-scenario validation protocol} that quantifies model
  mismatch impact and auto-correction effectiveness via the recovery
  ratio~$\rho$.

\item \textbf{Validation across 36 modalities} demonstrating 100\%
  compilation pass rate and 86.1\% canonical chain fidelity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Constrained Primitive Compiler: Six-Gate Validation}

The compiler validates agent outputs through 6 sequential gates
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\textbf{Gate~1 --- DAG compilation.}\quad The agent's FlowchartElement
list is translated into an \texttt{OperatorGraphSpec} (typed Pydantic~v2
model) and compiled via the \texttt{GraphCompiler}, which validates DAG
acyclicity, primitive ID existence, and shape compatibility.

\textbf{Gate~2 --- Canonical chain validation.}\quad The compiled
operator's canonical chain (sequence of \texttt{CanonicalPrimitive}
enums) is extracted and compared against the modality registry of 36
known decompositions.  Example: CT should produce $\Pi \to D$; CASSI
should produce $M \to W \to \Sigma \to D$.

\textbf{Gate~3 --- Complexity bounds.}\quad Node count~$N$ and DAG
depth~$D$ are checked against the FPB bounds $N_\text{max} = 20$ and
$D_\text{max} = 10$.

\textbf{Gate~4 --- Nonlinear constraints.}\quad For each Detect~(D) and
Transform~($\Lambda$) node, the compiler verifies that (a)~the node's
family is one of the 5 canonical families, and (b)~all parameters are
within physics-based bounds (Table~\ref{tab:nonlinear}).  Lipschitz
constants are computed where available.

\textbf{Gate~5 --- Adjoint consistency.}\quad For fully-linear operator
graphs, the randomised dot-product test verifies
$\langle Ax, y \rangle = \langle x, A^T y \rangle$
to relative tolerance~$10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Gate~6 --- Representation error (optional).}\quad When a
reference operator $\Atrue$ is available, the compiler estimates
\[
  \eps = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\|\Aagent(x) - \Atrue(x)\|}{\|\Atrue(x)\|}\right]
\]
over random test vectors.

\subsection{Three-Agent Pipeline}

The system design pipeline consists of three specialised agents:

\textbf{Plan Agent} receives a natural language description and generates
a structured JSON specification containing:
(a)~a list of \texttt{FlowchartElement}s with physical parameters, noise
sources, and mismatch specifications;
(b)~an ASCII flowchart showing the signal path;
(c)~measurement shape and noise model.

\textbf{Judge Agent} evaluates the Plan Agent's output for physical and
algorithmic feasibility.  The Judge receives both the LLM-generated
analysis \emph{and} the Constrained Primitive Compiler's validation
report.  Compiler failures are surfaced as critical issues.

\textbf{Performance Agent} analyses expected metrics (measurement SNR,
reconstruction PSNR/SSIM, computational cost) and compares against
published benchmarks for the modality.

The pipeline supports iterative refinement: if the Judge rejects a
design, the Plan Agent receives the specific failure reasons and
generates an updated specification (up to 3 rounds).

\subsection{Four-Scenario Validation Protocol}

To quantify the impact of model mismatch between the agent-designed and
true imaging systems, we define a 4-scenario protocol:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Four-scenario validation protocol.  Each scenario uses a
different combination of forward model and reconstruction.}
\label{tab:scenarios}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}clll@{}}
\toprule
Scenario & Forward Model & Reconstruction & Meaning \\
\midrule
I   & $\Atrue$                    & Optimal        & Upper bound (no mismatch) \\
II  & $\Aagent$ (mismatched) & Same as I      & Lower bound (full mismatch) \\
III & $\Atrue$ (oracle)     & Using $\Atrue$ & Oracle reference \\
IV  & $\Aagent$ + auto-correction & No oracle      & Practical correction \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The \textbf{recovery ratio} is defined as
\[
  \rho = \frac{\text{PSNR}_\text{IV} - \text{PSNR}_\text{II}}
              {\text{PSNR}_\text{I} - \text{PSNR}_\text{II}}\,,
\]
where $\rho = 1$ indicates full recovery of the mismatch gap and
$\rho \geq 0.5$ is considered acceptable.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{From Natural Language to Formal Guarantees}

The key insight of this work is that constraining LLM generation to a
proven-complete primitive basis transforms the agent design problem from
an open-ended generation task into a search over a structured space with
formal properties.  The FPB Theorem guarantees that this space is
\emph{complete} (any imaging forward model can be expressed), while the
Constrained Primitive Compiler guarantees that every point in this space
is \emph{valid} (satisfies physical constraints and error bounds).

This is analogous to how type systems in programming languages prevent
certain classes of bugs by construction: our compiler prevents physically
invalid imaging system designs \emph{by construction}.

\subsection{Comparison with Related Work}

\textbf{AI for scientific discovery.}\quad
AlphaFold\cite{jumper2021alphafold} navigates the space of protein folds;
GNoME\cite{merchant2023gnome} discovers new materials; our system
navigates the space of imaging forward models.  The key difference is
that our search space has a \emph{proven-complete basis}, enabling
formal error guarantees that are absent in data-driven discovery.

\textbf{LLM agents for engineering.}\quad
Recent work has applied LLMs to circuit design, drug discovery, and
materials synthesis.  Our work is distinguished by the formal verification
layer (the compiler) that bridges LLM output and physical validity.

\textbf{Computational imaging frameworks.}\quad
Existing frameworks (SigPy\cite{ong2019sigpy}, ODL\cite{adler2017odl},
DeepInverse\cite{ongie2020deep}) provide operators but not automated
design.  Our system complements these by providing the design layer
that precedes operator instantiation.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Tier-2 fidelity.}\quad The FPB Theorem operates at Tier-2
  (linear, shift-variant) fidelity.  Full-wave or Monte Carlo effects
  (Tier-3/4) are not captured and may require additional correction.

\item \textbf{Reconstruction quality depends on algorithm.}\quad
  The compiler validates the forward model, not the reconstruction
  algorithm.  Poor algorithm choice can degrade quality even with a
  valid forward model.

\item \textbf{LLM hallucination risk.}\quad While the compiler catches
  physically invalid designs, it cannot catch designs that are valid but
  inappropriate for the user's actual application.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Directions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Closed-loop experimental validation.}\quad Deploy
  agent-designed systems on real hardware and compare measured data
  with predictions.

\item \textbf{Automatic reconstruction algorithm selection.}\quad Extend
  the Performance Agent to select optimal reconstruction algorithms from
  a catalogue matched to the canonical chain structure.

\item \textbf{Transfer learning across modalities.}\quad Use the
  canonical chain structure to transfer design knowledge between related
  modalities (e.g., $M \to C \to D$ pattern shared by SIM, STED, and
  PALM/STORM).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Agent-to-Graph Translation}

The \texttt{AgentToGraphTranslator} maps each \texttt{FlowchartElement}
(produced by the Plan Agent) to one or more \texttt{GraphNode}s with
canonical primitive assignments.  The translator uses deterministic
keyword-based rules:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Source elements} are mapped by carrier type (X-ray
  $\to$ \texttt{xray\_source}, photon $\to$ \texttt{photon\_source}, etc.)
\item \textbf{Interaction elements} are mapped by model hint
  (\texttt{beer\_lambert} $\to \Lambda$, \texttt{absorption} $\to M$,
  \texttt{scatter} $\to R$, etc.)
\item \textbf{Geometry elements} are mapped to transport primitives
  (\texttt{radon} $\to \Pi$, \texttt{kspace} $\to F$,
  \texttt{fresnel} $\to P$, \texttt{psf} $\to C$, etc.)
\item \textbf{Detector elements} are mapped to~D with family detection
  (\texttt{intensity} $\to$ square-law,
  \texttt{interferometric} $\to$ coherent-field, etc.)
\end{itemize}

The translator also: (a)~auto-detects the physical carrier from source
elements; (b)~builds edges from \texttt{connects\_to} fields or creates
a sequential chain; (c)~appends a noise terminal node if missing.

\subsection{Nonlinear Constraint Validation}

For each nonlinear primitive node (D, R, $\Lambda$), the compiler:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Resolves the family enum (e.g., \texttt{DetectFamily.intensity\_square\_law})
\item Retrieves the \texttt{NonlinearFamilyConstraint} dataclass specifying
  allowed parameter names and physics-based bounds
\item Validates that: (a)~the number of declared parameters is $\leq 2$;
  (b)~each parameter value falls within the physics-based bounds
\item Computes the Lipschitz constant where the bound is input-independent
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Adjoint Consistency Test}

For fully-linear operator graphs, the compiler verifies the adjoint using
the randomised dot-product test:
\[
  \delta = \frac{|\langle Ax, y \rangle - \langle x, A^T y \rangle|}
               {\max(|\langle Ax, y \rangle|, \eps)}
\]
with 3 random trials and tolerance $10^{-4}$.  This ensures the adjoint
implementation is correct, which is critical for iterative reconstruction
algorithms that rely on $A^T$ for gradient computation.

\subsection{Representation Error Estimation}

Given a reference operator $\Atrue$, the compiler estimates:
\[
  \eps = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\|\Aagent(x) - \Atrue(x)\|}{\|\Atrue(x)\|}\right]
\]
by averaging over 5 random non-negative test vectors.  A threshold of
$\eps < 0.01$ is used, matching the FPB Theorem bound.

\subsection{Four-Scenario Protocol Implementation}

Each scenario applies a specific combination of forward model and
reconstruction:

\begin{align}
  \text{Scenario I:}\quad & y = \Atrue(x) + n; \quad
    \hat{x} = \arg\min_x \|\Atrue(x) - y\|^2 + \lambda\,\mathrm{reg}(x) \label{eq:s1}\\
  \text{Scenario II:}\quad & y = \Atrue(x) + n; \quad
    \hat{x} = \arg\min_x \|\Aagent(x) - y\|^2 + \lambda\,\mathrm{reg}(x) \label{eq:s2}\\
  \text{Scenario III:}\quad & \text{Oracle: initialise from II, refine with } \Atrue \label{eq:s3}\\
  \text{Scenario IV:}\quad & \text{Auto-correction: data-consistency refinement with } \Aagent \label{eq:s4}
\end{align}

The default reconstruction uses projected gradient descent with
non-negativity constraint.  Custom reconstruction functions can be
plugged in for modality-specific algorithms (e.g., filtered back-projection
for CT, SENSE for MRI, ePIE for ptychography).

\subsection{Implementation Details}

The entire framework is implemented in Python with:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Type system:} Pydantic~v2 with \texttt{StrictBaseModel}
  (\texttt{extra="forbid"}, NaN/Inf rejection)
\item \textbf{Primitives:} 101 implementations covering all 11 canonical types
\item \textbf{Compiler:} 6-gate pipeline with $\sim$0.35\,ms mean compilation time
\item \textbf{Test suite:} 45 tests covering constraints, translation,
  compilation, metrics, and end-to-end integration
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proof: Bounded Parametrisation Prevents Universal Approximation}

The key property of the nonlinear constraint system is that each of the
5 Detect families and 5 Transform families has at most 2 free parameters.
A function class with $k$ parameters over a bounded domain has
VC~dimension at most $k+1$.  Since $k \leq 2$ for all families, no
single primitive can approximate an arbitrary continuous function (which
would require VC~dimension $\to \infty$ by the universal approximation
theorem).  This bounded VC~dimension is what makes the 11-primitive
basis \emph{falsifiable}: if a modality requires a nonlinearity outside
the 10 canonical families, the basis fails --- and this failure is
\emph{detectable by the compiler}.

\subsection{Complexity Analysis}

The 6-gate compilation pipeline has the following complexity:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Gate~1 (DAG): $O(V + E)$ via Kahn's algorithm
\item Gate~2 (Chain): $O(V)$ extraction, $O(K)$ registry lookup
\item Gate~3 (Bounds): $O(1)$
\item Gate~4 (Nonlinear): $O(V)$ scan
\item Gate~5 (Adjoint): $O(T \cdot C_\text{fwd+adj})$ where $T$ = trials
\item Gate~6 (Error): $O(N \cdot C_\text{fwd})$ where $N$ = test vectors
\end{itemize}
Total: $O(V + E + T \cdot C_\text{fwd})$, dominated by the forward
model cost in Gates~5--6.
