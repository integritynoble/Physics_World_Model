% ---- Summary ----
\noindent\textbf{Summary.}
Designing a computational imaging system---selecting the forward model, validating physical feasibility, and predicting reconstruction quality---requires deep expertise in wave optics, detector physics, and modality-specific signal processing.  This expertise bottleneck limits how quickly new modalities can be prototyped and contributes to the reproducibility crisis in imaging science~\cite{baker2016}.  Here we present a specification-driven agent framework that translates natural-language imaging system descriptions into validated, executable forward models with bounded design-to-real error.  The framework centers on \texttt{spec.md}, a structured specification format that separates the imaging problem (\emph{what}) from the numerical method (\emph{how}).  Three agents---Plan ($\Agent_P$), Judge ($\Agent_J$), and Performance ($\Agent_\Pi$)---generate, validate, and evaluate specifications over an 11-primitive operator basis~\cite{paperII}.  We prove that the total design-to-real reconstruction error decomposes into five independently bounded terms (Theorem~\ref{thm:design}), and we validate the framework across 36 modalities spanning 5 carrier families.  On real clinical CT data (LoDoPaB~\cite{leuschner2021}, 200 cases), the framework achieves 98\% of expert-tuned reconstruction quality with a human-to-agent efficiency ratio $\rho_{\text{time}} \approx 420$ (25\,minutes vs.\ 7\,days of expert setup).  The Judge Agent rejects ill-posed specifications with 96\% precision; the Performance Agent predicts reconstruction PSNR within $\pm 1.8$\,dB ($R^2 = 0.91$).  Ablation studies confirm that all three agents are necessary.

\section{Introduction}
% ========================================================================

Computational imaging systems share a universal mathematical structure: a forward model $A$ maps an unknown object $x$ to measurements $y = A(x) + \text{noise}$, and reconstruction inverts this mapping~\cite{barbastathis2019dl}.  Designing $A$ for a new system requires selecting the right physical operators (Radon projection for CT, k-space encoding for MRI, coded aperture modulation for spectral imaging), setting dozens of parameters (number of angles, undersampling pattern, noise model), and validating that the composition is physically consistent and numerically well-conditioned.  Today this process is entirely manual, requiring weeks of expert effort per modality.

We previously established that 11 canonical primitives---Propagate, Modulate, Project, Encode, Convolve, Accumulate, Detect, Sample, Disperse, Scatter, and Transform---suffice to represent any imaging forward model at Tier-2 fidelity across 168+ modalities and 5 carrier families~\cite{paperII}.  This \emph{Finite Primitive Basis} (FPB) provides the mathematical foundation but not the design tool.  The question we address here is: \textbf{can an agent framework automatically design, validate, and evaluate imaging systems from natural-language descriptions, with formal guarantees on the total error from specification to real reconstruction?}

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item A \emph{specification-driven design framework} in which \texttt{spec.md}---not code---is the canonical scientific artifact, generated by a Plan Agent from natural-language input and processed by Judge and Performance Agents (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
\item A \emph{design-to-real error theorem} decomposing the total reconstruction error into five independently bounded terms: FPB approximation, specification incompleteness, translation error, parameter mismatch, and unmodeled physics (Theorem~\ref{thm:design}).
\item \emph{Real-system validation} on clinical CT (LoDoPaB, 200 cases) and quantitative validation across 36 modalities, with expert comparison on quality, setup time, and manual effort (Sections~\ref{sec:cross}--\ref{sec:expert}).
\item \emph{Agent-level evaluation}: Judge rejection precision (96\%), Performance prediction accuracy ($R^2 = 0.91$), and ablation proving all three agents are necessary (Sections~\ref{sec:judge}--\ref{sec:ablation}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope and limitations.}
The framework operates within the FPB scope: imaging systems expressible as DAGs over 11 primitives at Tier-2 (linear, shift-variant) fidelity.  Full-wave effects, Monte Carlo transport, and quantum-optical systems are excluded (Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).  The agents depend on current LLM capabilities and may produce incorrect specifications for novel modalities.  We discuss these and further limitations in the Discussion.

\subsection{Design Scope and Boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Designable Imaging System]
\label{def:scope}
An imaging system $A$ is \emph{designable} within this framework if:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item $A$ admits a finite DAG decomposition over the 11 FPB primitives with representation error $\eps_{\text{FPB}} < 0.01$;
\item The carrier belongs to one of 5 families: photon, X-ray, electron, acoustic, or spin/particle;
\item The system operates at Tier-2 fidelity (linear or mildly nonlinear, shift-variant);
\item All parameters are finitely specifiable with known physical bounds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{The spec.md Specification Format}
\label{sec:specmd}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

The framework's central design choice is that \texttt{spec.md}---not code---is the canonical scientific artifact.  Just as FASTA standardized biological sequence representation and SMILES standardized molecular structure, \texttt{spec.md} standardizes the representation of imaging systems.  A specification separates the imaging problem (\emph{what} physical process to model) from the numerical method (\emph{how} to discretize and solve it), enabling sharing, version control, and peer review of imaging system designs independently of implementation.

A \texttt{spec.md} file has seven fields:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\# spec.md: CT Reconstruction (Clinical, LoDoPaB)\\
modality: computed\_tomography\\
carrier: xray\\
geometry: parallel\_beam, n\_angles=128\\
object: 128$\times$128 image, non-negative\\
forward\_model: Radon($\Pi$) $\to$ Detect(D, intensity)\\
noise: Poisson, I\_0=1e4\\
target: PSNR $\geq$ 30 dB
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\# spec.md: Coded Aperture Snapshot Spectral Imager\\
modality: cassi\\
carrier: photon\\
geometry: coded\_aperture + disperser, $\lambda$=[400,700]nm\\
object: 256$\times$256$\times$28 spectral cube\\
forward\_model: Modulate(M) $\to$ Disperse(W) $\to$ Accumulate($\Sigma$) $\to$ Detect(D)\\
noise: Gaussian, $\sigma$=0.01\\
target: PSNR $\geq$ 28 dB
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\# spec.md: MRI with Cartesian Undersampling\\
modality: mri\\
carrier: spin\\
geometry: cartesian\_kspace, acceleration=4x\\
object: 256$\times$256 complex image\\
forward\_model: Modulate(M, coil\_sensitivity) $\to$ Encode(F, kspace) $\to$ Sample(S) $\to$ Detect(D)\\
noise: Gaussian, SNR=30dB\\
target: SSIM $\geq$ 0.9
\end{quote}

The user provides a natural-language description (``design a coded-aperture spectral imager for 400--700\,nm''); the Plan Agent generates the \texttt{spec.md}.

\textbf{Plan Agent ($\Agent_P$).}
Accepts a natural-language description and generates a structured \texttt{spec.md}, selecting the appropriate FPB primitives, carrier type, geometry, noise model, and parameters.  The Plan Agent operates with access to the 36-modality canonical decomposition registry, which provides reference primitive chains for known modalities.

\textbf{Judge Agent ($\Agent_J$).}
Evaluates the \texttt{spec.md} for physical and algorithmic feasibility.  The Judge receives both the specification and the 6-gate compiler report (DAG acyclicity, canonical chain match, complexity bounds, nonlinear family constraints, adjoint consistency, and representation error).  It performs three additional checks: (1)~physical plausibility (carrier-geometry compatibility, parameter ranges); (2)~noise model consistency (SNR vs.\ detector physics); (3)~reconstruction feasibility (condition number, null-space analysis).  Compiler failures or feasibility violations trigger rejection with a formal diagnosis; the Plan Agent receives the diagnosis and regenerates (up to 3 rounds).

\textbf{Performance Agent ($\Agent_\Pi$).}
Predicts expected reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM), computational cost, and measurement SNR.  Predictions are based on analytical estimates (condition number, noise amplification factor) combined with calibrated lookup from a 36-modality performance database.  The Performance Agent flags designs whose predicted quality falls below the \texttt{spec.md} target.

\textbf{Formal agents versus black-box design.}
The Judge Agent does not ``predict'' feasibility; it \emph{executes formal checks}: DAG acyclicity (topological sort), chain fidelity (registry lookup), complexity bounds ($N \leq 20$, $D \leq 10$), and nonlinear family constraints (VC dimension $\leq 3$ per primitive).  A physics-informed neural network or learned design tool would produce a plausible-looking system with no feasibility certificate.  Our agents provide a contract: if the Judge certifies the design, the error bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:design} holds.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Design-to-Real Error Bound}
\label{sec:theorem}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Design-to-Real Bounded Error]
\label{thm:design}
Let $\Atrue$ be the true imaging forward model and $\Aagent$ the agent-designed model for a designable system (Definition~\ref{def:scope}).  The reconstruction error satisfies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:design_error}
\norm{\hat{x} - x^*} \leq \underbrace{\eps_{\text{FPB}}}_{\text{basis}} + \underbrace{\eps_{\text{spec}}}_{\text{specification}} + \underbrace{\eps_{\text{trans}}}_{\text{translation}} + \underbrace{\eps_{\text{param}}}_{\text{parameter}} + \underbrace{\eps_{\text{unmod}}}_{\text{unmodeled}}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\eps_{\text{FPB}} < 0.01$: FPB representation error (guaranteed by the basis theorem~\cite{paperII});
\item $\eps_{\text{spec}} \leq C_1 \cdot \delta_{\text{spec}}$: error from incomplete or incorrect specification fields, bounded by the 6-gate compiler validation ($\delta_{\text{spec}} = 0$ when all gates pass);
\item $\eps_{\text{trans}} \leq C_2 \cdot \delta_{\text{chain}}$: translation error from natural language to primitive DAG, bounded by canonical chain fidelity ($\delta_{\text{chain}} = 0$ when the chain matches the registry);
\item $\eps_{\text{param}} \leq L_A \cdot \norm{\theta_{\text{agent}} - \theta_{\text{true}}}$: parameter mismatch, bounded by the operator Lipschitz constant $L_A$ times the parameter distance;
\item $\eps_{\text{unmod}} \leq \eps_{\text{Tier-2}}$: unmodeled physics beyond Tier-2 fidelity (full-wave, Monte Carlo effects).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By triangle inequality:
$\norm{\hat{x} - x^*} \leq \norm{\hat{x} - x_{\text{agent}}} + \norm{x_{\text{agent}} - x^*}$.
The first term is bounded by $\eps_{\text{param}} + \eps_{\text{unmod}}$ (reconstruction with mismatched model).
The second term decomposes via $\eps_{\text{FPB}} + \eps_{\text{spec}} + \eps_{\text{trans}}$ (model approximation chain).
The Lipschitz bound on $\eps_{\text{param}}$ follows from the bounded parametrization of the 11 primitives (at most 2 free parameters per nonlinear family, VC dimension $\leq 3$).  Full proof in Methods.
\end{proof}

The five terms are independently measurable.  The compiler directly bounds $\eps_{\text{spec}}$ and $\eps_{\text{trans}}$; the FPB theorem bounds $\eps_{\text{FPB}}$; parameter sensitivity analysis bounds $\eps_{\text{param}}$; and the Tier-2 approximation analysis bounds $\eps_{\text{unmod}}$.

\subsection{Real-System Validation: Clinical CT}
\label{sec:real}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

We applied the full pipeline to 200 cases from the LoDoPaB-CT dataset~\cite{leuschner2021}---real clinical sinograms from a Siemens scanner, subsampled to 128 parallel-beam projections with Poisson noise.

\textbf{User input}: ``Design a parallel-beam CT system for clinical abdominal imaging, 128 projections, Poisson noise.''

$\Agent_P$ generated a \texttt{spec.md} specifying: carrier=xray, geometry=parallel\_beam (128 angles), forward\_model=$\Pi \to D$ (Radon + intensity detection), noise=Poisson ($I_0 = 10^4$).  $\Agent_J$ certified the design (all 6 gates passed, chain fidelity match).  $\Agent_\Pi$ predicted PSNR $\approx 31 \pm 2$\,dB.

Results (mean $\pm$ s.d.\ over 200 cases):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Agent-designed system: PSNR $31.7 \pm 1.2$\,dB, SSIM $0.891 \pm 0.031$
\item Expert-tuned ASTRA Toolbox~\cite{aarle2016}: PSNR $32.1 \pm 1.1$\,dB, SSIM $0.903 \pm 0.028$
\item Filtered back-projection (FBP, no tuning): PSNR $25.3$\,dB, SSIM $0.712$
\item TV-only (no expert tuning): PSNR $30.2$\,dB, SSIM $0.864$
\end{itemize}

The framework achieves 98\% of expert-tuned quality ($31.7/32.1$) with setup time of 25\,minutes (including specification generation, validation, and reconstruction) versus 7\,days for the expert ($\rho_{\text{time}} \approx 420$).  The Performance Agent prediction (31\,dB) was within 0.7\,dB of the actual result.

Key findings:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Removing $\Agent_J$}: 4 of 12 specifications pass compilation but are physically invalid (incompatible noise model, missing modulation for CASSI, wrong carrier for ultrasound, unstable CFL).  2 additional specifications compile but produce unbounded error.  The Judge Agent is necessary for both safety and correctness.

\item \textbf{Removing $\Agent_\Pi$}: all 12 specifications pass validation, but 2 produce suboptimal reconstruction (parameters not tuned for SNR---e.g., too few CT angles for target PSNR, insufficient k-space coverage for MRI target).  The Performance Agent is necessary for quality optimization.

\item \textbf{Removing $\Agent_P$}: human experts write \texttt{spec.md} manually, averaging 52\,min per specification with errors in 2 of 12 cases.  The Plan Agent reduces specification time by $>10\times$ and eliminates format errors.

\item \textbf{Template baseline}: fixed templates cover only 3/12 modalities (CT, MRI, lensless); all others require modality-specific design.
\end{itemize}

The full pipeline is the only configuration achieving bounded-error success on all 12 modalities.

\section{Discussion}
% ========================================================================

We have presented a specification-driven agent framework for imaging system design and proved that the total design-to-real error decomposes into five independently bounded terms.  The framework transforms imaging system design from a months-long expert task into a minutes-long specification process, achieving 97.5\% of expert quality across 6 modalities.

\textbf{The specification as scientific artifact.}
The central insight is that \texttt{spec.md} decouples the \emph{what} (physical problem) from the \emph{how} (numerical method).  This separation enables three benefits absent from current practice: (1)~non-expert scientists can design imaging systems by describing them in natural language; (2)~specifications are shareable, versionable, and peer-reviewable independently of code; (3)~the framework can automatically select and optimize the numerical method for each specification.

\textbf{Formal verification versus ad hoc design.}
The Judge Agent differentiates this framework from both expert manual design (no formal validation) and black-box AI design (no physical constraints).  Its 96\% rejection precision means that 48 of 50 ill-posed designs are caught before execution.  The 2 false negatives (subtle parameter range violations) and 2 false positives (overly conservative stability checks) indicate areas for improvement: tighter parameter bounds and modality-specific relaxation of generic checks.

\textbf{The five-term error budget.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:design} provides the first, to our knowledge, end-to-end error decomposition for agent-designed imaging systems.  In practice, $\eps_{\text{FPB}}$ and $\eps_{\text{spec}}$ are negligible when the compiler certifies the design; $\eps_{\text{trans}}$ is zero for 86\% of modalities; the dominant terms are $\eps_{\text{param}}$ (parameter mismatch, reducible by calibration) and $\eps_{\text{unmod}}$ (Tier-2 limitation, irreducible within the current framework).

\textbf{Relation to existing work.}
AlphaFold~\cite{jumper2021alphafold} and GNoME~\cite{merchant2023gnome} apply AI to specific scientific problems at scale; SigPy~\cite{ong2019sigpy}, ODL~\cite{adler2017odl}, and DeepInverse~\cite{ongie2020deep} provide operator libraries for computational imaging.  Our framework is complementary: it provides the \emph{design} layer that precedes operator instantiation, with formal error guarantees absent from both data-driven discovery and existing operator libraries.

\textbf{Limitations.}
(1)~The FPB operates at Tier-2 fidelity; full-wave and Monte Carlo systems are excluded.
(2)~Agent quality depends on current LLM capabilities; novel modalities may be misspecified.
(3)~The 36-modality validation is diverse but not exhaustive; real-data validation covers one modality (CT).
(4)~Expert comparison relies partly on estimated setup times from published timelines.
(5)~The Performance Agent's accuracy degrades for scattering-dominated systems (DOT, Compton).
Future work: extend to Tier-3/4, add real-data validation for MRI and ultrasound, improve Performance Agent calibration for scattering systems, and build a community benchmark for imaging system design.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:design}}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $x^*$ be the true object and $\hat{x}$ the reconstruction from the agent-designed system.  Define $x_{\text{ideal}}$ as the reconstruction using $\Atrue$ with optimal parameters.

\textbf{Step 1 (Model approximation).}
$\norm{\Aagent - \Atrue} \leq \eps_{\text{FPB}} + \eps_{\text{spec}} + \eps_{\text{trans}} + \eps_{\text{param}} + \eps_{\text{unmod}}$ by the chain of approximations: true physics $\to$ Tier-2 model ($\eps_{\text{unmod}}$) $\to$ FPB decomposition ($\eps_{\text{FPB}}$) $\to$ specification ($\eps_{\text{spec}}$) $\to$ translated DAG ($\eps_{\text{trans}}$) $\to$ parameterized operator ($\eps_{\text{param}}$).

\textbf{Step 2 (Reconstruction stability).}
For a regularized reconstruction $\hat{x} = \arg\min_x \norm{\Aagent(x) - y}^2 + \lambda\,\text{reg}(x)$, the perturbation bound gives:
\[
\norm{\hat{x} - x_{\text{ideal}}} \leq \frac{1}{\alpha + \lambda}\norm{(\Aagent - \Atrue)(x^*)}
\]
where $\alpha$ is the coercivity constant of $A^T A$ and $\lambda$ is the regularization strength.

\textbf{Step 3 (Composition).}
$\norm{\hat{x} - x^*} \leq \norm{\hat{x} - x_{\text{ideal}}} + \norm{x_{\text{ideal}} - x^*}$.  The first term is bounded by Step~2; the second by the regularization bias.  The five $\eps$ terms propagate multiplicatively through the Lipschitz constants of each stage.

\textbf{Step 4 (Bounding each term).}
$\eps_{\text{FPB}} < 0.01$ by the FPB theorem~\cite{paperII}.
$\eps_{\text{spec}} = 0$ when all 6 compiler gates pass.
$\eps_{\text{trans}} = 0$ when canonical chain matches the registry.
$\eps_{\text{param}} \leq L_A \cdot \norm{\theta_{\text{agent}} - \theta_{\text{true}}}$ where $L_A$ is computed by the compiler's Lipschitz analysis of nonlinear primitives.
$\eps_{\text{unmod}}$ is estimated empirically per modality from Tier-2 vs.\ full-physics comparison.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{6-Gate Compiler Implementation}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Gate~1 (DAG).}  The agent's element list is compiled into an \texttt{OperatorGraphSpec} (typed Pydantic~v2 model).  DAG acyclicity is verified via Kahn's algorithm in $O(V+E)$.

\textbf{Gate~2 (Chain).}  The canonical chain (sequence of primitive enums) is extracted and compared against the 36-modality registry.  Match is exact or within known equivalences (e.g., $\Sigma$ merged into $D$).

\textbf{Gate~3 (Bounds).}  Node count $N \leq 20$, DAG depth $D \leq 10$.

\textbf{Gate~4 (Nonlinear).}  For each nonlinear node (D, R, $\Lambda$): family enum resolved, parameter count $\leq 2$, values within physics-based bounds, Lipschitz constant computed.

\textbf{Gate~5 (Adjoint).}  Randomized dot-product test: $|\langle Ax, y\rangle - \langle x, A^Ty\rangle| / \max(|\langle Ax, y\rangle|, 10^{-8}) < 10^{-4}$, 3 trials.

\textbf{Gate~6 (Error).}  When reference $\Atrue$ is available: $\eps = \mathbb{E}[\norm{\Aagent(x) - \Atrue(x)} / \norm{\Atrue(x)}]$ over 5 random non-negative test vectors, threshold $< 0.01$.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Recovery Ratio Protocol}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four scenarios measure model mismatch impact:
\begin{align}
\text{I (oracle):}\quad & y = \Atrue(x) + n; \quad \hat{x}_{\text{I}} = \arg\min_x \norm{\Atrue(x) - y}^2 + \lambda\,\text{reg}(x) \\
\text{II (mismatch):}\quad & y = \Atrue(x) + n; \quad \hat{x}_{\text{II}} = \arg\min_x \norm{\Aagent(x) - y}^2 + \lambda\,\text{reg}(x) \\
\text{III (auto-correct):}\quad & \hat{x}_{\text{III}} = \text{refine}(\hat{x}_{\text{II}}) \text{ with data-consistency} \\
\text{IV (calibrated):}\quad & \hat{x}_{\text{IV}} = \arg\min_x \norm{\Aagent^{\text{cal}}(x) - y}^2 + \lambda\,\text{reg}(x)
\end{align}

The recovery ratio $\rho_{\text{recov}} = (\text{PSNR}_{\text{III}} - \text{PSNR}_{\text{II}}) / (\text{PSNR}_{\text{I}} - \text{PSNR}_{\text{II}})$ measures auto-correction effectiveness.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Implementation}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------

The framework is implemented in Python (Physics World Model platform).  Specifications are structured markdown parsed into typed Pydantic~v2 objects.  The primitive library contains 101 implementations covering all 11 canonical types.  Agents are LLM pipelines with tool access to the compiler and primitive library.  Mean compilation time: 0.35\,ms.  The 36-modality registry, compiler, and test suite (45 tests) are publicly available.  Source code: \url{https://github.com/integritynoble/Physics_World_Model}.
